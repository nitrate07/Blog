# Arı Kaynak — Article Template

Reusable structure for a new case file. Fill in every section; delete none without a reason (if a section genuinely doesn't apply — e.g. no additional sources exist — say so explicitly rather than omitting it silently).

---

## TITLE

A specific, honest headline. Not "Does X Cause Y?" clickbait — state what's actually being checked.

## FILE METADATA

- File No.: [next sequential number, e.g. 0009]
- Filed: [date]
- Category: [one of: Exercise / Health / Longevity / Nutrition / Supplements / Women's Health — or a new category if genuinely needed]
- Evidence type: [see EDITORIAL_STANDARD.md hierarchy]
- Evidence strength: Strong / Moderate / Limited

## CLAIM

The exact claim as it's circulating, quoted as closely as fair use allows. This is what a reader saw or heard — not a paraphrase that's already been softened toward accuracy.

## SOURCE OF CLAIM

Where the claim came from (a specific video, post, or public figure) — described, not necessarily linked if it's ephemeral social content.

## VERDICT

One of: Supported / Mostly Supported / Partly Supported / Misleading / Unsupported / False
(See EDITORIAL_STANDARD.md for the precise definition of each tier.)

## WHY THIS MATTERS

1–2 sentences on why a reader should care — what decision or belief this claim might actually affect.

## WHAT THE RESEARCH ACTUALLY FOUND

The substantive body of the article. Walk through the primary source's actual methodology and findings in plain language. This is where most of the word count lives.

## EVIDENCE QUALITY

- Study type (RCT, cohort, meta-analysis, animal study, position statement, etc.)
- Sample size, if applicable
- Whether it's been replicated
- Who conducted/funded it, if relevant to interpreting the result

## LIMITATIONS

Limitations the source's own authors acknowledge, plus any additional context needed (small n, short duration, animal-only, single study, specific population that may not generalize).

## WHAT THE STUDY DOES NOT SHOW

Explicitly state the boundary — what a reader might assume the study proves that it actually doesn't. This is often the single most useful sentence in the file.

## BOTTOM LINE

2–3 sentences: what's the honest, practical takeaway for someone who read the viral version and now wants the real one.

## PRIMARY SOURCE

- Journal name
- Publication date
- Authors
- DOI and/or PMID (verify these resolve — never invent one)
- Direct link

## ADDITIONAL SOURCES

Any supporting citations (established physiology, prior related studies, guideline documents). Same sourcing standard as the primary source — no secondary blogs as scientific backing.

## RELATED INVESTIGATIONS

Link only to files that are genuinely related by topic, evidence type, or claim structure — not filler links.

## LAST UPDATED

Date of most recent substantive change. If none since filing, state the filing date.

---

## Bilingual requirement

Every file ships in both English and Turkish. The Turkish version is a full editorial translation — not machine-literal — and must preserve:
- The exact verdict
- The exact evidence strength rating
- All limitations and caveats
- All source citations (DOI/PMID/journal names unchanged)

## Before publishing, confirm

- [ ] Every citation (DOI, PMID, journal, author names) has been independently verified to exist and say what it's cited as saying
- [ ] The verdict evaluates the claim's wording, not just the topic's legitimacy
- [ ] Animal/observational findings are labeled as such, not presented as settled human causation
- [ ] The EN and TR versions match in verdict, evidence strength, and substance
- [ ] Category is assigned and matches an existing taxonomy entry (or a new one is deliberately introduced sitewide)
- [ ] Internal links (related files, breadcrumb, homepage archive entry) all resolve
