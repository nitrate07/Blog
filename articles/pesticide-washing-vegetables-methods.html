Does Just Rinsing Under Running Water Really Remove 77-84% of Pesticides?

The core finding is real and correctly directionally described. The specific top number is a touch higher than what the study itself measured.

Evidence typeControlled comparative laboratory study
Evidence strengthHigh4/5 ●●●●○

The claim

A post argues you don't need chemical vegetable washes to reduce pesticide exposure. It cites a Korean laboratory study that deliberately contaminated leafy greens with 23 different pesticides, then compared household washing methods, finding that simply soaking briefly in still water followed by rinsing under running water while rubbing by hand removed pesticides at a 77-84% rate.

Original source

The post cites: "Güney Kore Gwangju Enstitüsü (Foods 2022 Makalesi)."

Scientific source

This is real and precisely matches. Yang, Mun, Kim, and colleagues at the Health and Environment Research Institute of Gwangju, South Korea, published "Effectiveness of Different Washing Strategies on Pesticide Residue Removal: The First Comparative Study on Leafy Vegetables" in Foods in 2022 (DOI: 10.3390/foods11182916). The study artificially contaminated five leafy vegetables (lettuce, perilla leaves, spinach, crown daisy, and ssamchoo) and tested nine different washing/removal methods.

What the study actually found

MethodPesticide reduction
Running water77.0% (highest of all methods tested)
Boiling59.5%
Detergent43.7% (lowest)
Overall range across methods43.7% – 77.0%

Running water was indeed the single most effective method tested, well ahead of detergent-based washing. This directly supports the post's core, practical message: you don't need special products, just thorough rinsing under running water.

Where the numbers slightly diverge

The post states a range of "77-84%," but the study's actual top result was 77.0% for running water, and the overall method range topped out at 77.0% as well (individual vegetable-level results ranged 40.6-67.4% across the five vegetables tested). This file could not identify an 84% figure within this specific study. It's possible the post is drawing the upper figure from a different, related washing-method study — several similar comparative studies exist internationally with somewhat different results — but as cited, the number runs slightly ahead of what this particular paper reports.

What this doesn't mean

  • No washing method removes 100% of pesticide residue — even the best-performing method left roughly a quarter of measured residue behind.
  • Results varied meaningfully by vegetable and by specific pesticide — lettuce responded best to washing (67.4% reduction), while ssamchoo responded worst (40.6%), and some pesticides resisted removal by boiling or blanching specifically.

The verdict

This file is stamped Mostly Supported. The study is real, correctly identified, and its central finding — running water outperforms detergent and other methods for pesticide removal on leafy greens — is accurately conveyed. The precision gap: the specific "77-84%" range slightly exceeds the study's actual reported top figure of 77.0%.
