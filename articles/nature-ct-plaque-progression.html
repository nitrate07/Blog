Does "Non-Dangerous" Plaque Really Double in Size Over 5 Years?

The study behind this claim is real and addresses a genuine blind spot in cardiology. Some of the specific figures in the post couldn't be independently confirmed digit-by-digit.

Evidence typeRetrospective cohort study
Evidence strengthModerate3/5 ●●●○○

The claim

A post argues that "non-obstructive" or soft coronary plaque — often dismissed as not dangerous because it doesn't significantly narrow the artery — isn't actually harmless. It cites a new study following people with no history of major cardiac events and no cholesterol-lowering medication, finding that their soft plaque volume roughly doubled over about 5 years, and that the share of "low-attenuation plaque" (a marker associated with higher risk) rose substantially over the same period.

Original source

The post cites: "Aldana-Bitar J, Krishnan S, Ichikawa K, et al. 'NATURal history of coronary PlaquE on cardiac computed tomography in individuals without MACE or lipid-lowering therapy: NATURE-CT study.' Journal of Cardiovascular Computed Tomography, May 2026."

Scientific source

This study is real and current. It was published in the Journal of Cardiovascular Computed Tomography in May 2026 by a team including Jairo Aldana-Bitar, Matthew J. Budoff, and Ronald P. Karlsberg, based at the Lundquist Institute for Biomedical Innovation at Harbor-UCLA Medical Center and the Cardiovascular Research Foundation of Southern California. It retrospectively identified 205 participants from two outpatient cardiovascular imaging centers in Los Angeles, all of whom had at least two CT angiography scans at least two years apart, no prior major cardiac events, and no lipid-lowering therapy during the study window.

The core motivation behind the study is a real, meaningful gap in cardiology research: most studies of how coronary plaque changes over time follow people who already have risk factors, prior events, or are on treatment — this study specifically looked at people without any of those, to understand the natural, untreated course of the disease.

What this file could and couldn't verify

  • Confirmed: the study exists, is current, is peer-reviewed, and studies exactly the population and question described in the post — plaque progression in people without prior cardiac events or treatment.
  • Confirmed: the study's core motivation matches the post's framing — challenging the assumption that non-obstructive plaque in low-risk-appearing people is simply not worth monitoring.
  • Not independently confirmed: the specific granular numbers in the post — the mean patient age, average LDL and triglyceride levels, exact BMI, the precise doubling figure for soft plaque volume, and the exact percentage shift in low-attenuation plaque. This file's research could confirm the study's existence, design, and population size (205 participants, two Los Angeles sites) but could not pull the exact figures from the abstract text available.

Why this matters, and how we're handling it

Rather than either presenting these specific numbers as confirmed or ignoring the claim entirely, this file discloses the gap directly: the surrounding science and study design check out, but the precise figures should be treated as reported-but-not-independently-verified until this file can be updated with a direct read of the study's full results tables.

The verdict

This file is stamped Mostly Supported. The study is real, current, peer-reviewed, and addresses exactly the population and question the post describes — a genuine, underexplored corner of cardiology research. The precision gap here is about verification completeness rather than factual overreach: several of the post's specific numbers could not be independently confirmed against the full text within this review.
