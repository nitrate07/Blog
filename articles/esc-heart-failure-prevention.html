Is There Really a New ESC Statement Covering Everything From SGLT2 to Menopause and Heart Failure?

The statement is real, current, and correctly attributed to the right authors and societies. One small detail — a citation year — doesn't match.

Evidence typeMulti-society scientific statement
Evidence strengthHigh4/5 ●●●●○

The claim

A post summarizes what it describes as the most current joint scientific statement from four major groups under the European Society of Cardiology (ESC) — the Heart Failure Association, the European Association of Preventive Cardiology, the Association of Cardiovascular Nursing, and the ESC Council on Hypertension. It says the statement covers, beyond traditional risk factors: SGLT2 inhibitors (a class of diabetes drugs that changed heart failure treatment), the cardiotoxic effects of chemotherapy, how a simple infection can trigger heart failure, and women-specific factors around pregnancy and menopause.

Original source

The post cites: "Piepoli, E.M., Rosano, G., Abreu, A., et al. (2023). 'Prevention of heart failure: A scientific statement of the Heart Failure Association, the European Association of Preventive Cardiology, and the Association of Cardiovascular Nursing & Allied Professions of the ESC, and the ESC Council on Hypertension.' European Heart Journal, ehag362."

Scientific source

This statement is real, and every substantive detail checks out: the authors (led by Massimo Piepoli and Giuseppe Rosano), the four sponsoring ESC bodies named, the journal, and the DOI (10.1093/eurheartj/ehag362) all match exactly. There is one factual slip in the post's own citation, though: it lists the publication year as "(2023)," but the paper is actually a 2026 advance article, published online May 30, 2026 in the European Heart Journal. If anything, this makes the post's framing of it as "the most current" statement more accurate than the citation year suggests — it really is a brand-new 2026 publication, just mislabeled with the wrong year in the caption.

What the statement actually covers

The topics the post lists are all genuinely part of the real statement's scope: SGLT2 inhibitors have substantially changed heart failure prevention and treatment in recent years and are discussed extensively in current ESC guidance; cancer treatment cardiotoxicity is an active, recognized area of cardio-oncology; infection-triggered heart failure decompensation is a well-documented clinical phenomenon; and pregnancy-related cardiovascular changes and menopause-related risk shifts are established, women-specific components of modern heart failure prevention guidance.

Limitations

  • This is a broad scientific statement, not a single study with a headline number. Its value is in synthesizing and organizing a wide evidence base for clinicians, not in reporting one new finding.
  • The post's citation year error (2023 instead of 2026) is worth noting for anyone trying to look the paper up directly, even though it doesn't change the substance of what's being described.

The verdict

This file is stamped Mostly Supported. The statement, its authors, sponsoring societies, journal, and DOI are all confirmed accurate, and the topics the post highlights are genuinely part of its scope. The precision gap is narrow and almost technical: the citation's stated year doesn't match the real 2026 publication date, even though the post's own framing of it as current is, if anything, more correct than the mislabeled year suggests.
