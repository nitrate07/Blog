Is Uncontrolled Blood Pressure Really Behind Most Aortic Dissections?

A genuinely rare, dramatic condition, examined with a genuinely large, long-running dataset. The specific "75-85%" figure needed a closer look.

Evidence typePooled prospective cohort study
Evidence strengthHigh4/5 ●●●●○

The claim

A post examines aortic dissection — a tear in the aorta's wall, one of cardiology's most feared, fastest-developing emergencies. It cites a new American Journal of Preventive Cardiology study combining two long-running cohorts (ARIC and MESA), tracking over 22,000 people for 30 years, and lists uncontrolled hypertension as the single biggest culprit, present in 75-85% of cases and responsible for more than half of dissections on its own; active smoking as roughly doubling dissection risk; and age and male sex as major non-modifiable factors.

Original source

The post cites: "Erhabor, J., Dardari, Z., Matsushita, K., Whelton, S., Blankstein, R., Yao, Z., Tarwidhi, E., ... 'Incidence and risk factors for aortic dissection in a community-based cohorts: The Atherosclerosis Risk in Communities (ARIC) and Multi-Ethnic Study of Atherosclerosis (MESA) studies.' American Journal of Preventive Cardiology, 2026, 101665."

Scientific source

This is real and current. Erhabor, Dardari, Matsushita, Whelton, Blankstein, and colleagues published this exact study in the American Journal of Preventive Cardiology in 2026 (DOI: 10.1016/j.ajpc.2026.101665). The study pooled the ARIC and MESA cohorts, following 22,171 adults without baseline aortic disease (mean age 56.6, 54% female) for a median of 20.2 years — slightly over 20 years rather than the "30 years" the post states, though ARIC and MESA as parent studies together span a longer overall era, which likely explains the discrepancy. During follow-up, 97 incident aortic dissection cases occurred.

What the study actually found

Risk factorWhat the study found
HypertensionMajor driver; researchers used Cox regression to confirm it as a leading modifiable risk factor
Active smokingMeaningfully elevated risk in regression analysis
Age and male sexConfirmed as major non-modifiable risk factors
Study designA four-variable risk score (age, sex, systolic blood pressure category, smoking) was developed from these findings

The study's authors used the findings to build a simple four-variable risk score, aimed at helping identify which people might benefit most from closer monitoring or more aggressive blood pressure control — matching the post's framing that this data can inform real prevention strategy, not just describe risk after the fact.

Limitations

  • Aortic dissection is rare — only 97 cases among 22,171 people over two decades — so while the cohort is large, the number of actual dissection events driving the risk-factor analysis is comparatively small, which is typical and expected for a rare-disease study but worth keeping in mind.
  • The exact "75-85% of cases" and "responsible for more than half" figures reflect well-established aortic dissection literature broadly (hypertension is consistently the top-named risk factor across studies of this condition), but this file could not confirm those precise percentages appear as stated within this specific 2026 paper's own results section.
  • The "30 years" of follow-up in the post is somewhat imprecise — the study itself reports a median follow-up of 20.2 years for the pooled analysis.

The verdict

This file is stamped Mostly Supported. The study, its authors, cohort sizes, and core conclusions — hypertension and smoking as major modifiable risk factors, age and male sex as major non-modifiable ones — are all accurately represented. The precision gap: the specific "75-85%" and "more than half" figures, while consistent with the broader aortic dissection literature, weren't independently confirmed as appearing exactly that way in this paper, and the follow-up duration is stated a bit more generously than the study's own 20.2-year median.
